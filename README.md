## Target model and capacity

All 2026 figures are assumptions or simulated operating records. Selling starts 15 January. Use 10 weeks to prospect, propose and sign, then collect through 7 April. Four reps supply 40 budgeted rep-weeks across the campaign, with 36 allocated and four held back. The assumed slot cap is 42 and the spend cap is ₹1.50 lakh.

| Stage or assumption | 2025 source | Base | Stretch |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Prospects | 180 unique brands touched | 140 | 160 |
| Held conversations | 60 | 80 (57.1%) | 100 (62.5%) |
| Sent proposals | 46 distinct opportunities ever proposed | 65 (81.3% of meetings) | 84 (84.0%) |
| Signed cash deals | 20 | 32 (49.2% of proposals) | 40 (47.6%) |
| Weighted mean cash deal | ₹1,00,000 | ₹1,25,000 | ₹1,36,875 |

| Expected collection by cutoff | Unknown | 88% | 92% |
| **Pre-fest cash** | **Unknown** | **₹35.20L** | **₹50.37L** |

The base assumes 65 proposals from 80 meetings. If only 60% of meetings reach proposal, the team sends 48 proposals, leaving a gap of 17. The commercial bet is that a 24-hour proposal turnaround prevents some of the historical handoff loss. The biggest volume dependency is securing 14 introduction-sourced cash deals, up from six. The stretch requires 10 cash deals per rep and uses 40 of 42 slots. Review sponsor cash budget and usable inventory before holding a slot.

The model assumes every signed sponsor pays a 60% advance by cutoff. At base, 70% of the remaining 40% arrives on time, giving 60% + 40% × 70% = 88%. At stretch, 80% of the balance arrives, giving 92%. The week-four pilot explicitly tests whether the advance assumption is credible.

**Downside check.** Holding other assumptions fixed, if just 80% of advance rupees arrive and 70% of balance rupees arrive, base cash falls to ₹40 lakh × (60% × 80% + 40% × 70%) = **₹30.40 lakh**. The ₹4.80 lakh shortfall is large enough to justify dedicating a rep to finance follow-up.

## How the dashboard and analyst work

**History and operations.** The archive and simulated ledger are separate. All calculations use INR. Open proposed value is pipeline, confirmed cash value is signed, and posted receipts determine collected cash. At week four the ledger covers 64 prospects, 41 meetings, 27 proposals and eight signed cash deals. Its cost and task figures are simulated. Operating date and receipt cutoff are editable. A campaign update creates or revises a dated checkpoint. Budget and owner edits apply to the working campaign.

**Current-pipeline forecast.** For each acquisition campaign before cutoff, forecast the greater of signed cash × assumed collection rate and cash already received. Add unsigned proposals × scenario close rate × observed mean deal size × collection rate. Use planned mean price when there are no signed deals. At or after cutoff use eligible posted receipts only. This view measures what current business might collect; the full festival target also requires future prospects. The forecast assumes unsigned proposals remain viable and does not model individual lost proposals, close dates or shared market shocks.

**Embedded analyst.** Local mode computes answers over current ledger state, workbook IDs, point-in-time history and a term-weighted index of workbook entities and field definitions. It supports follow-ups, comparisons, rankings, lookup, missing-data answers and confirmation before a task is added. Its parser has a finite vocabulary. An unrecognised question may return matching rows or ask for a more specific metric. This is retrieval and calculation over supplied data; no foundation model was fine-tuned.

**Optional Gemini query planning.** In Model settings, supply a key and a model ID available to it. For eligible questions the service receives the question, recent chat context, available fields and one sample row per table. It proposes a read-only query. The browser validates columns, filters and operations and computes the result locally. Operational decisions and safeguards still use the local rules. The key stays in tab memory and clears on reload. Live provider access requires the reviewer's own key, network and plan; the core demo works without them. [Gemini model availability](https://ai.google.dev/api/models) · [GenerateContent API](https://ai.google.dev/api/generate-content)

## Verification and limitations

The workbook audit used Python, openpyxl and pandas. The dashboard uses browser JavaScript, HTML and CSS. ReportLab produced the PDF. OpenAI Codex assisted with auditing, implementation, rewriting and testing. The submitted draft supplied the city, sponsorship track, core channel ideas, static-app concept and optional Gemini design. The historical data, operating ledger, target math and decision rules were rebuilt for this version. The local demo has no hosted inference charge.

The repository includes a Node regression suite. Run `node --test tests/analytics.test.cjs` with a current Node runtime. It checks source deduplication, historical dates and goals, opportunity IDs, receipts, cutoff exclusion, threshold changes, contextual follow-ups and the structured query validator. Browser checks exercise the full diagnosis-to-task flow and a saved payment after reload. Live Gemini authentication was not exercised with a paid or personal API key. Browser-local storage is for a single reviewer. It has no shared accounts or bank connection. The workbook cannot establish causal attendance uplift or historical cash receipts.
